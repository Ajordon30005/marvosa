## The Core Idea: Compose, Don't Invent

A memory that **learns from experience** needs three things, and all three are
already present across the GUHCT skills. The skill's only contribution is to
**arrange** them:

|----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Potentiation (LTP) | `hcl-pure` COMP | `COMP(term, term)` — same phase sector, constructive, amplitude grows, identity preserved, no new term |
| Depression (LTD) | `virtual-memory-hcl` decay | `SHIFT` by η halves unaccessed traces; faded ones prune at the noise floor |

| Integrity | all three | the α self-check (≈137) on every engine |

> [!NOTE]
> **Metaphor Transparency:** **LTP** (Long-Term Potentiation) and **LTD** (Long-Term Depression) are neuroscience metaphors used for conceptual clarity. The operational specification is strictly technical: LTP is `COMP(term, term)` — strengthen in place, constructive — and LTD is decay, `SHIFT(term, η)` — halve.

## Why Experience Matters (the design principle)

A fresh store with all amplitudes equal cannot prioritize — recall is flat.
Structure emerges only when **experience** (which memories are accessed) and a
**self-preservation signal** (which memories matter to acting) drive LTP/LTD
over many cycles. Reinforced traces grow into dominant single memories; unused
ones fade and prune. Diversity of stored memories plus repeated, consequential
access is what turns the undifferentiated substrate into a system that ranks
what matters above noise. This is demonstrable, not asserted — run the demo.
